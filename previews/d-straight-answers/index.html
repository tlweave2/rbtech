Your questions

Common questions.

Water

Will it use Riverbank's water?

No Riverbank city water.

Cooling uses closed-loop, air-cooled chillers with a one-time charge of recycled, non-drinkable industrial water from existing on-site wells, not municipal drinking or agricultural water supplies.

For scale: the one-time charge is consistent with the annual needs of a few homes.

Power bills

Will it raise my electric bill?

No strain on the local grid.

Electricity is sourced from outside the Riverbank area, using renewable hydroelectric and other clean power, over grid infrastructure built for the site's former heavy industry. RTC does not raise electricity costs for Riverbank ratepayers.

Who pays: the project will pay for any onsite utility upgrades, not ratepayers or taxpayers.

Noise

Will I hear it?

Below the levels already allowed.

All computing equipment is housed indoors in existing large industrial buildings, and all equipment sits more than 200 feet from the street. Low-decibel chillers keep noise below the levels already allowed by the industrial setting.

Air

Will it pollute the air?

No turbines or generators running.

Clean grid power for day-to-day operation. No large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power. Any onsite power is designed to minimize local air-quality impact.

Land

Is it taking farmland or housing land?

No. It's already industrial.

Built on land already zoned for industrial use, in existing buildings that once supported an aluminum smelter and U.S. Army ammunition casings production. Not agricultural land or land that could be used for housing.

Neighbors: one of 37 existing tenants at the complex.

Size

How big is it?

Medium-sized. Not a mega data center.

A modular computing facility housed inside existing buildings, with no unsightly new structures built for computing.

How it works

The SCF is economic development that adds jobs, repeatable tax revenues, and a sustainable project that doesn't pollute or deplete natural resources. Riverbank Technology Center is the first project built to the SCF standard.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

What is a Sustainable Computing Facility (SCF)?

Economic development that adds jobs, repeatable tax revenues, and a sustainable project that doesn't pollute or deplete natural resources: zero continuous water usage for cooling, sustainable power sourcing, reuse of existing industrial buildings, industrially-zoned land rather than agricultural land, and large, recurring local tax revenue. Riverbank Technology Center is the first project built to the SCF standard.

Where exactly is it?

Inside the existing Riverbank Industrial Complex, 5300 Claus Rd, Riverbank, CA 95367. Open in Google Maps.

What will the facility be used for?

It is a modern computing facility built to house high-performance computing.

Where does the electricity come from?

From outside the Riverbank area, using renewable hydroelectric and other clean power, reusing grid infrastructure originally built for the site's former heavy industry. RTC does not strain local grid capacity or raise electricity costs for Riverbank ratepayers.

Where does the cooling water come from?

Cooling water is recycled, non-drinkable industrial water drawn from existing on-site wells, paired with closed-loop, air-cooled chillers. No City of Riverbank drinking water is used. The one-time charge is consistent with the annual needs of a few homes, and there is zero continuous water usage for cooling.

Will there be generators or gas turbines?

No large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power. Day-to-day operation uses clean grid power, and any onsite power is designed to minimize local air-quality impact.
