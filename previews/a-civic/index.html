Riverbank Industrial Complex · Riverbank, California

A quiet computing facility, inside buildings Riverbank already has.

Riverbank Technology Center is a joint project between Aemetis and Spartan, establishing the first Sustainable Computing Facility in the US at the existing Riverbank Industrial Complex. All computing equipment will be housed indoors in existing large industrial buildings, with no Riverbank city water, no strain on the local grid, and no turbines or generators running.

A new standard

A Sustainable Computing Facility.

Aemetis, in partnership with Spartan, calls it the Sustainable Computing Facility, or SCF: economic development that adds jobs, repeatable tax revenues, and a sustainable project that doesn't pollute or deplete natural resources.

  • Zero continuous water usage for cooling. A one-time charge of water consistent with the annual needs of a few homes, using recycled on-site non-potable water.
  • Sustainable power sourcing. Existing grid and transmission capacity already built to serve the site, rather than new capacity.
  • Reuse of existing industrial buildings. No new construction, with noise below the amount allowed by existing regulations.
  • Industrially-zoned land, not agricultural land. Underused industrial property put to work, not farmland or land that could be used to build housing.
  • Large, recurring local tax revenue. Sustained property tax that funds schools, local governments, fire protection, libraries, and parks.

Riverbank Technology Center is the first project built to the SCF standard.

The project

A computing facility inside the Riverbank Industrial Complex.

A modern computing facility built to house high-performance computing, inside the existing Riverbank Industrial Complex, reusing site resources rather than starting from raw land.

  • Reuse: housed inside existing buildings that once supported an aluminum smelter and U.S. Army ammunition casings production, not new construction.
  • Setback: all equipment sits more than 200 feet from the street, with no new structures built for computing.
  • Zoning: land already zoned for industrial use, not agricultural land or land that could be used for housing.
  • Scale: one of 37 existing tenants at the complex. A modular, medium-sized computing facility, not a mega data center.

Sustainable by design

Water-wise cooling. Clean, quiet power.

Sustainability isn't an add-on here: it's the design. We're building to be a good neighbor: protecting Riverbank's water, air, and local grid, keeping noise low, and putting the resources the site already has to their best use.

Water

  • Zero continuous water usage for cooling.
  • Recycled, non-drinkable industrial water drawn from existing on-site wells, paired with closed-loop, air-cooled chillers.
  • No City of Riverbank drinking water, and no agricultural water supplies.
  • The one-time charge of water is consistent with the annual needs of a few homes.

Power

  • Sourced from outside the Riverbank area, using renewable hydroelectric and other clean power.
  • Reuses grid infrastructure originally built for the site's former heavy industry.
  • Does not strain local grid capacity or raise electricity costs for Riverbank ratepayers.
  • The project will pay for any onsite utility upgrades the facility requires, not ratepayers or taxpayers.
  • No large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power. Any onsite power is designed to minimize local air-quality impact.

FAQ

Straight answers.

What is a "Sustainable Computing Facility"?

It's economic development that adds jobs, repeatable tax revenues, and a sustainable project that doesn't pollute or deplete natural resources. An SCF has zero continuous water usage for cooling, sustainable power sourcing, reuse of existing industrial buildings, industrially-zoned land rather than agricultural land, and large, recurring local tax revenue. Riverbank Technology Center is the first project built to the SCF standard.

How much water will it use, and where does it come from?

There is zero continuous water usage for cooling. The closed-loop system needs a one-time charge of water consistent with the annual needs of a few homes. That water is recycled, non-drinkable industrial water drawn from existing on-site wells, not municipal drinking or agricultural water supplies.

Will my electricity rates go up?

RTC does not strain local grid capacity or raise electricity costs for Riverbank ratepayers. It uses existing grid and transmission capacity already built to serve the site, and the project will pay for any onsite utility upgrades the facility requires, not ratepayers or taxpayers.

How loud will it be?

All computing equipment will be housed indoors in existing large industrial buildings, more than 200 feet from the street. There are no large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power, and low-decibel chillers keep noise below the levels already allowed by the industrial setting.

Why the Riverbank Industrial Complex?

An SCF puts underused industrial property to work, rather than converting farmland or land that could be used to build housing. The site already has existing industrial buildings and grid infrastructure originally built for its former heavy industry.

Who are Aemetis and Spartan?

Aemetis is a leading developer and producer of renewable fuels and low-carbon energy, and has been master developer of the Riverbank Industrial Complex since 2022. Spartan designs and delivers modular computing facilities.
