Riverbank Industrial Complex · 5300 Claus Rd

A quiet computing facility, inside buildings Riverbank already has.

Riverbank Technology Center (RTC) would put high-performance computers inside existing industrial buildings at the former Army ammunition plant. It would not use city drinking water, would not require new local grid capacity, and would not run generators or gas turbines day to day.

Responsible Development Commitments

Seven promises, and how you can hold us to them.

These commitments are written for neighbors, Riverbank city leaders, and Stanislaus County officials. Each one says what we promise and how you can check that we're keeping it.

  1. Protect the local grid and electricity rates

    We promise: RTC uses existing transmission capacity and pays for any on-site utility upgrades it needs.

    How to check: Utility interconnection agreements and upgrade costs are filed with the city.

  2. Use water responsibly

    We promise: RTC uses no City of Riverbank water. The cooling loop gets a one-time fill of recycled, non-drinkable water from on-site wells.

    How to check: We'll publish metered well-water use every year.

  3. Keep operations clean and quiet

    We promise: All equipment stays indoors, chillers are low-noise, and no turbines or generators run day to day.

    How to check: We'll publish noise readings at the property line.

  4. Design with neighbors in mind, on existing industrial land

    We promise: RTC reuses existing buildings more than 200 ft from the street, on land already zoned industrial.

    How to check: The site plan is on file with the city.

  5. Engage regularly with the community

    We promise: We'll hold regular public updates, and a named contact will answer every question.

    How to check: The meeting schedule and past Q&A are posted below.

  6. Explain community benefits clearly

    We promise: We'll publish plain-language figures on taxes, jobs, and local spending.

    How to check: The sources behind every number are listed in our commitments PDF.

  7. Follow through on our commitments

    We promise: We'll report publicly every year on each commitment above.

    How to check: Annual reports will be posted here.

The site

Inside the Riverbank Industrial Complex.

The complex once housed an aluminum smelter and U.S. Army ammunition casing production. Aemetis has been its master developer since 2022. RTC would occupy existing buildings there and become one of 37 tenants.

  • Reuse: equipment goes inside existing buildings, with no new structures for computing.
  • Setback: all equipment sits more than 200 ft from the street.
  • Zoning: the land is already zoned industrial, so no farmland or housing land is used.
  • Scale: a modular, medium-sized facility.

Sustainable by design

How the water and power work.

Water: filled once, then reused

  • No City of Riverbank drinking water
  • No agricultural water
  • The one-time fill is about the same as a few homes use in a year

Power: existing lines, clean sources

  • Power comes from outside the Riverbank area, including renewable hydroelectric.
  • It uses grid and transmission capacity originally built for the site's heavy industry.
  • RTC pays for any on-site utility upgrades. Ratepayers and taxpayers don't.
  • No gas turbines or diesel generators run day to day.
  • RTC can supply excess power back to the grid.

FAQ

Straight answers.

What exactly is a "Sustainable Computing Facility"?

It's our name for a computing facility built to five standards: no continuous cooling water use, power from existing grid capacity, reuse of existing buildings, industrial (not agricultural) land, and recurring local tax revenue. RTC would be the first one built to that standard.

How much water will it use, and where does it come from?

The closed cooling loop gets a one-time fill of recycled, non-drinkable industrial water from existing on-site wells. Those wells are not connected to Riverbank's municipal system. After the fill, the water recirculates. There is no continuous draw for cooling.

Will my electricity rates go up?

RTC uses transmission capacity already built for the site's former heavy industry and pays for any on-site utility upgrades it needs. It doesn't ask ratepayers or taxpayers to fund new capacity.

How loud will it be?

All computing equipment is indoors, more than 200 ft from the street. The chillers are low-noise, and no turbines or generators run day to day. Noise will stay below the levels already allowed in this industrial zone.

Will there be backup generators?

No generators or gas turbines run for day-to-day power. Any on-site power is designed to minimize local air-quality impact.

Why Riverbank?

The complex already has large industrial buildings, industrial zoning, and grid connections that are underused. Putting them to work avoids building on farmland or land that could be used for housing.

How many jobs, and are they local?

Building RTC would create hundreds of construction jobs, followed by lasting local service jobs during operations.

Who are Aemetis and Spartan?

Aemetis produces renewable fuels and low-carbon energy and has been master developer of the Riverbank Industrial Complex since 2022. Spartan designs and delivers modular computing facilities.

Didn't the city pause data center projects?

Yes. In September 2026 the City Council adopted a temporary moratorium so it could study data center proposals. We support a careful review, and we've published our commitments so the Council and residents can evaluate this project on its specifics.
