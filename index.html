The project

A computing facility inside the Riverbank Industrial Complex.

A modern, modular computing facility that will reuse existing buildings and land at the Riverbank Industrial Complex, putting a portion of the site back to work for a clean, quiet purpose.

  1. Reuse

    Existing structures that once housed an aluminum smelter and U.S. Army ammunition casings production.

  2. Setback

    All computing equipment will be located inside existing buildings, more than 200 feet from the street.

  3. Zoning

    Land already zoned for industrial use rather than agricultural. No farmland converted.

  4. Scale

    One of the more than 30 tenants at the complex. A modular, medium-sized computing facility, not a mega data center.

Sustainable by design

Water, power, and noise.

Much of the national concern around data center development is rooted in legitimate questions about water use, noise, pollution, and utility rates. A Sustainable Computing Facility is designed to address these concerns directly.

  1. Water

    Zero continuous water usage for cooling. Closed-loop systems recirculate cooling water, much like the radiator in a car. Water for the initial fill and maintenance is minimal, about the quantity of a typical community swimming pool, and comes from non-drinkable industrial water drawn from on-site wells. No City of Riverbank utility water will be sourced or impacted.

  2. Power

    Sourced from the California grid outside the Riverbank area, drawing on hydroelectric and other sources, using existing grid and transmission capacity built for the site's prior heavy industrial uses. RTC will pay for any utility upgrades the project requires, so it does not raise local electricity prices for Riverbank households and businesses.

  3. Quiet

    No reliance on large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power. Any potential future onsite power systems are designed to minimize local air-quality impact. With low-noise chillers, noise stays below levels already allowed by the industrial setting.

The five parts of the Sustainable Computing Facility standard
TopicThe Sustainable Computing Facility (SCF) standard
WaterZero continuous water usage for cooling
PowerSustainable power sourcing from non-local power production, using existing grid and transmission capacity
BuildingsReuse of existing industrial buildings
LandIndustrially-zoned land, not agricultural land
Tax revenueLarge, recurring local tax revenue

An SCF is a second-generation approach to delivering computing services. Riverbank Technology Center will be our first project built to the SCF standard.

What is a "Sustainable Computing Facility"?

A second-generation approach to delivering computing services, defined by five criteria: zero continuous water usage for cooling, sustainable power sourcing, reuse of existing industrial buildings, industrially-zoned land rather than agricultural land, and large, recurring local tax revenue. Riverbank Technology Center will be our first project built to the SCF standard.

How much water will it use, and where does it come from?

There is zero continuous water usage for cooling. Closed-loop systems recirculate cooling water, much like a car radiator. Water for the initial fill and maintenance is minimal, about the quantity of a typical community swimming pool, and comes from non-drinkable industrial water drawn from on-site wells, not Riverbank's drinking water supply.

Will my electricity rates go up?

RTC will pay for any utility upgrades the project requires, so it does not raise local electricity prices for Riverbank households and businesses. Electricity will be sourced from the California grid outside the Riverbank area, using existing grid and transmission capacity built for the site's prior heavy industrial uses.

How loud will it be?

All computing equipment will be located inside existing buildings, more than 200 feet from the street. RTC will not rely on large, loud generators or gas turbines for day-to-day power, and with low-noise chillers, noise will stay below levels already allowed by the industrial setting.

Why the Riverbank Industrial Complex?

Its power and grid infrastructure was originally built to serve heavy industrial production, first as an aluminum reduction (smelting) plant and later for ammunition production. An SCF puts underused industrial property to work rather than converting farmland.

Who are Aemetis and Spartan?

Aemetis is a leading developer and producer of renewable fuels and low-carbon energy. It became master developer of the Riverbank Industrial Complex in 2022 and is one of the largest property taxpayers in Stanislaus County. Spartan brings engineering and delivery experience to design and build modern, modular computing facilities.
